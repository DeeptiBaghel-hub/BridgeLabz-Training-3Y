A City Frozen Between Eras

Kyoto served as Japan's capital for over a thousand years, and it still carries that history in its wooden machiya houses, quiet temple gardens, and narrow stone streets.

Temples & Shrines

With over 1,600 Buddhist temples and 400 Shinto shrines, Kyoto rewards slow wandering. Fushimi Inari's endless orange gates and Kinkaku-ji's golden pavilion are the icons, but the smaller, unnamed shrines tucked into alleyways are just as memorable.

Kaiseki & Street Food

From multi-course kaiseki dinners to yatsuhashi sweets sold in Nishiki Market, Kyoto's food scene moves between refined tradition and casual snacking without missing a beat.

Four Very Different Seasons

Cherry blossoms line the Philosopher's Path in spring, while maple trees turn Arashiyama into deep reds and oranges every November. Winter brings quiet snow-dusted gardens few tourists see.

Getting Around

Kyoto's bus and subway network reaches most major sights, but renting a bicycle is often the best way to experience the city at street level.
